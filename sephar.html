SEPHAR: Underwater Glider for Europa

Design and analysis of an autonomous underwater glider for the subsurface ocean of Europa (Jupiter). BSc thesis in Aerospace Engineering, Universidad Europea de Madrid.

Mission

Europa has a saltwater ocean up to about 100 km deep under its ice shell. A vehicle there has to withstand very high hydrostatic pressure, navigate without GPS or light and operate for months on a single battery.

Architecture

Buoyancy-driven glider with no propellers. It changes its buoyancy to sink and rise, and the hydrofoils turn that vertical motion into forward glide, so energy is only spent at each change of direction. Myring hull in CFRP, 2.70 m long and about 200 kg, with NACA 0009 hydrofoils.

FIG-02Cutaway: forward pressure vessel, battery banks, sliding pitch-control mass, aft electronics bay

Analysis

Geometry in Fusion 360, hydrodynamics in OpenFOAM (k-ω SST with Langtry-Menter transition) over a sweep of angles of attack, cross-checked with a panel method in XFLR5, and a longitudinal dynamics and mission model in MATLAB.

Results

  • Maximum lift-to-drag ratio of 4.84.
  • Longitudinally unstable by design (static margin −8.9 %), stabilised by active pitch control with a stepper-driven sliding battery mass.
  • Nominal mission of 6 full-depth dives over 74.9 days, using 3,483 Wh of an 8,400 Wh battery.
  • An alternate configuration for the deepest part of the ocean also met the mission objectives.
