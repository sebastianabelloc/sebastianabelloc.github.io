SEPHAR — Underwater Glider for Europa

My final degree project: the design and analysis of an autonomous submersible glider conceived to reach and profile the subsurface ocean of Europa — one of the Solar System's strongest candidates for life.

Mission

Europa hides a saltwater ocean up to ~100 km deep beneath its ice. Reaching it means surviving extreme hydrostatic pressure, operating with no GPS and no light, and enduring for months on a single battery — a design problem that breaks most conventional assumptions.

Architecture

A buoyancy-driven glider was selected — no propellers or rotating machinery in the flow. The vehicle changes its own buoyancy to sink and rise, while hydrofoils convert that vertical motion into forward glide, spending energy only at each dive inflection. Hull: a Myring body of revolution in CFRP, ~2.70 m long, ~200 kg, with NACA 0009 hydrofoils.

Analysis pipeline

Geometry parametrised in Fusion 360 → hydrodynamics characterised in OpenFOAM (k-ω SST with Langtry–Menter transition) across a sweep of angles of attack → cross-checked against a panel method in XFLR5 → longitudinal dynamic and mission model in MATLAB. Every mission value is traceable back to the computation that produced it.

Key results

  • Maximum lift-to-drag ratio of 4.84.
  • Longitudinally unstable by design (static margin −8.9%), stabilised with an active pitch-control system driven by a sliding internal mass.
  • Nominal mission: 6 full-depth dives over 74.9 days, using 3,483 Wh of an 8,400 Wh pack.
  • An alternate configuration tuned for the deepest part of the ocean also met mission objectives.
  • Complete engineering cycle — design, hydrodynamics, dynamics, control, validation — run on open-source tools on a laptop.
