Roadster Hop — 6-DOF GNC Simulation

When I saw the new Roadster lift off on cold-gas thrusters, roll over a lineup of cars and land, I wanted to know what it takes to fly that manoeuvre. So I wrote the dynamics, the controller and the guidance from scratch, and rendered the result in 3D.

The manoeuvre

  • Powered take-off: the 4 floor nozzles push at 1.9× the car's weight until it climbs at 7 m/s, then cut.
  • Full 360° barrel roll in 1.5 s over the lineup.
  • Braking and a 0.5 m hover, with the floor nozzles throttled to hold altitude.
  • Slow descent at 0.6 m/s and a level touchdown about 35 m after take-off.

Dynamics

A 2000 kg rigid body with a 13-state vector: position, velocity, attitude quaternion and body angular rates. Attitude is propagated with q̇ = ½ q·(0, ω) (Hamilton product) and the rotation with Euler's equation I ω̇ = τ − ω × I ω, integrated with RK45 and stopped by a touchdown event. Six nozzles — four under the floor and one on each side near the front wheels — are defined by their position and direction, and every force and torque comes from τ = Σ rᵢ × Fᵢ. None of them can push the car down.

Control

Attitude is held by a PD law on the quaternion error, qe = qref*·q, plus a feedforward of the torque the roll profile needs, so the feedback only corrects small errors. A control allocator then shares the commanded force and torques among the nozzles like the motors of a drone: the four floor nozzles are throttled between 0 and 1 for lift, roll and pitch, and the two side nozzles fire on/off with a deadband for yaw. The loop runs at 50 Hz with zero-order hold, like a flight computer.

Guidance

Each phase has its own reference: full lift until the cut-off speed, a smooth cosine roll profile from 0 to −360° with zero rate at both ends, a braking law vref = −√(2a(z − zhover)) that can always stop at the hover height, and an outer lateral loop that tilts the floor thrust slightly to bring the car back to the runway centre line.

Validation

  • 53 unit and closed-loop tests: the uncontrolled loop matches the plain free-flight integration, the roll ends level before braking, throttles stay within limits and the landing is soft.
  • A quick 2D preview of the trajectory in seconds, so the physics is checked before a 30-minute 3D render.
  • Max lateral drift of 1.2 m, well inside the runway lines.

Simplifications

Constant mass, ideal nozzles with instant thrust, no aerodynamics or ground effect and perfect state knowledge. Thrust levels and nozzle positions are my own estimates from the demo footage — this is a study of the manoeuvre, not a model of the real car. Not affiliated with Tesla.
